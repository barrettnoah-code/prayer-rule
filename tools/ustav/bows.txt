=== bows-rule | Bows: the rule of the Home Ustav

== At the entrance opening (fol. 149)
All these prostrations are made to the ground, except on Saturdays, Sundays and feast days, which were set out in Chapter 3 of this book. Likewise, throughout Pentecost and during the twelve days after Christ's Nativity, they are made as waist bows.

== On church and cell prostrations and kneeling: when they are made and when they are not. Chapter 2 (fol. 30v-38v)

== On prostrations in Great Lent
On Cheesefare Sunday at vespers, the entrance bows are waist bows. Prostrations to the ground begin at "Vouchsafe, O Lord." At "More honorable," four great prostrations. Then "Glory, both now," "Lord, have mercy" twice, "Lord, bless," and three prostrations to the ground. The departing bows are prostrations to the ground.
At small compline, the entrance bows are waist bows. Prostrations to the ground begin at "Glory to God in the highest." At "More honorable" we make only four prostrations.
On Monday of the first week, before the midnight office, the entrance and opening bows are three prostrations to the ground. During the midnight office itself all bows are waist bows, except four prostrations at "More honorable."
At the beginning of matins, bows are made from the waist. Likewise after the Six Psalms, at the Alleluia, three waist bows. All other bows at matins are prostrations to the ground. At the hours, the entrance and departing bows and all the rest are prostrations to the ground.
On Monday at vespers and great compline, and likewise on Tuesday at the midnight office, at matins and at the hours, all bows are prostrations to the ground as the typikon prescribes. So also on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, and throughout Lent, except on Saturdays, Sundays and feasts of saints. In all church and cell prayer, the bows are always prostrations to the ground.
On Fridays at vespers the bows are waist bows. The one exception is at the end of vespers, where three prostrations to the ground are made instead of seventeen. The departing bows are waist bows.
Compline on all Saturdays is great compline, except on Saturday of the fifth week, when it is small compline without prostrations.
On Saturday and Sunday there is no kneeling, either in church or in the cells. On Sunday at vespers the prostrations begin at "Vouchsafe, O Lord," and the rest is as described above for Cheesefare Sunday. At small compline, prostrations to the ground begin at "Glory to God in the highest." After that, at "O Thou who at all times and every hour," seventeen prostrations, and at the closing Trisagion three prostrations.
We sing the midnight office from the beginning to the end with prostrations, and the other services likewise with prostrations according to the typikon.
On Wednesday of the fifth week, compline is small compline without prostrations, except four great prostrations at "More honorable." The midnight office is the same.

== On Holy Week
On Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday of Holy Week, the bows throughout the church services are prostrations to the ground, as in Lent. On Wednesday at the hours the prostrations to the ground are dropped, and waist bows are made at vespers and throughout the services. But the entrance and departing bows, and those at the rule, are prostrations to the ground until the Resurrection service itself.

== On Bright Week
On holy and great Pascha Sunday, and throughout Bright Week, there are no prostrations at all, either in church or in the cell. In all the services and at the rule the bows are waist bows. The one exception is after "It is truly meet," and after "Shine, shine," where a prostration to the ground is made without fail.

== On Pentecost
*Great Typikon, and On the Christian Life.* From Thomas Sunday to the Descent of the Holy Ghost there is no fasting and no kneeling.
*Nikon of the Black Mountain, discourse 57.* On all Saturdays and Sundays, on feasts, on the twelve days, and on the days from Pascha to All Saints, no one is to bend the knee in prayer, only to bow simply. Kneeling begins again from All Saints.
*Solovetsky Typikon, ch. 30.* You should know that from Bright Sunday to All Saints' Sunday there are no prostrations to the ground, either in church or in the cells. Likewise there are no prostrations on any Saturday or Sunday, except Great Saturday. On that one Great Saturday, prostrations to the ground are made in the cells.

== On the other days of the whole year
*Nikon of the Black Mountain, discourse 57.* On the other days of the year, when there is no feast, prostrations end after the ninth hour on Friday and begin again on Monday, at "Vouchsafe, O Lord." In plain terms: when Alleluia is sung, prostrations are made. When "God is the Lord" is sung, there are no prostrations in church except the entrance and departing bows.
*Solovetsky Typikon, ch. 30.* On the other days, when there is a full peal of bells, the entrance and departing bows in church are waist bows, but in the cells all bows are prostrations to the ground as usual. When there is no full peal, the entrance and departing bows in church are prostrations to the ground, and those in the cells are prostrations to the ground as well. From Sunday evening into Monday, if there is no full peal, the entrance bows at vespers are waist bows and the departing bows are prostrations to the ground. The same applies at vespers of every all-night vigil: entrance and departing bows.
*Psalter printed under Patriarch Joseph: directions on entrance and departing bows.* On the eves of the Lord's feasts (Christ's Nativity and Theophany, the Transfiguration, the Exaltation of the Precious Cross), and of the Nativity of the God-bearer, the Entry, the Meeting and the Annunciation, the entrance and departing bows are the ordinary waist bows. The same applies from Christ's Nativity to Theophany, throughout Bright Week, and on the eves of Saturday and Sunday. It applies also to the feasts of the Forerunner (his Nativity and Beheading), to the holy apostles Peter and Paul, and to the patron saint of the church, and on all those days. After "It is truly meet," throughout the year, and in Bright Week after "Shine, shine," a great prostration to the ground is made without fail. Do the same throughout Pentecost, until All Saints' Sunday.
If the feast of a saint with a polyeleos falls, the entrance and departing bows are likewise waist bows. If the feast falls of a saint who has an entrance, Old Testament readings and the Great Doxology, then on the eves of those days the entrance and departing bows are waist bows. But on the forefeasts and afterfeasts of the Master's feasts and of the God-bearer, the entrance and departing bows, in the cell and in church, are prostrations to the ground.
*Great Typikon, ch. 32.* From Christ's Nativity to the day of holy Theophany there is no fasting and no kneeling. After the Baptism of the Lord, prostrations resume.

== On the fast-free week
In Artsiburius week the Armenians fast. But on all the days, Monday, Wednesday and Friday, we eat meat, cheese and eggs at the ninth hour. The entrance and departing bows, and those at the rule, are prostrations to the ground.

== On Cheesefare Week
In Cheesefare week, the bows are likewise prostrations to the ground. On Wednesday and Friday, at vespers, the midnight office, matins and the hours, all bows are prostrations to the ground, as in Great Lent, and compline is great compline.

== On great, middle and lesser feasts. Chapter 3 (fol. 39-44)
*Nikon of the Black Mountain, ch. 57, and the Great Typikon, ch. 60.* You should know that feasts are divided into three ranks: great, middle and lesser. The great feasts carry the sign of a cross within a circle [in the calendar: 🕀]. They are the feasts of Christ and of the God-bearer, two of the Forerunner (his Nativity and his Beheading), and those of both the all-praised apostles Peter and Paul and two of John the Theologian.
Middle feasts carry only a cross [in the calendar: 🕂, the polyeleos; the vigil, 🕁, is counted with them here].
Lesser feasts carry only three dots partly enclosed, in two forms [in the calendar: 🕃 in red, 🕄 in black].

== On lesser feasts
On lesser feasts that carry a red sign, there is no kneeling in church, and no entrance or departing prostrations, even in the middle of a fast. In private, both are made.
*Solovetsky Typikon, ch. 30.* On all other days when saints receive a full peal of bells, there are no prostrations to the ground in church, but in the cells all bows are prostrations to the ground as usual. At table we eat with oil and drink wine if the feast falls on a Wednesday or Friday outside a fast.
On those with the three dots in black, there is no kneeling in church, but the entrance and departing bows and the cell prostrations are prostrations to the ground. At table on such feasts, if they fall on a Wednesday or Friday, we eat cooked food without relish and drink wine. See the Great Typikon, ch. 32, and the same typikon, ch. 35.
On eating and drinking, as was observed, we are taught by the typikon of the Holy Mountain concerning lesser feasts, where it says that "God is the Lord" is sung. On eating and drinking, these feasts do not give release as other feasts do, nor again do they give it as fast days do. This applies when a fast day and a lesser feast fall together. *Nikon of the Black Mountain, discourse 57.*
In the fast of the holy apostles, according to the Typikon of the Holy Mountain, on lesser feasts we eat at the sixth hour and in the evening, but we do not eat fish. If the feast falls on a Wednesday or Friday, we do not drink wine. But if "God is the Lord" is sung, we eat at the sixth hour and in the evening, and if the hegumen commands, we drink wine. This is the pattern for lesser feasts.

== On middle feasts
*Typikon, ch. 60, and Nikon, discourse 57.* On middle feasts there are no prostrations at all in church. In private, in the cell, each does as he chooses, for that is a time of freedom.
*Solovetsky Typikon, ch. 1.* Where the crosses are not encircled, there are no prostrations to the ground in church on those days. In the cells, the fathers praise those who choose to make them and do not reproach those who do not. At table, if such feasts fall on a Wednesday or Friday outside a fast, we eat twice and drink wine. If a canon on eight is sung, we eat with oil. If the feast falls on a Monday, we eat fish and drink wine.
In the fast of the holy apostles, of Christ's Nativity and of the Dormition of the most holy God-bearer, we eat fish on such feasts if they fall on a Tuesday or Thursday. On a Monday we eat oil and drink wine, once a day. If it falls on a Wednesday or Friday, we eat once a day without oil, but drink wine. If the saint has a vigil, on Wednesday or Friday we eat with oil and drink wine, once a day. If it falls on a Monday, we eat fish and drink wine, once a day.
If the memory of the saint to whom the church and monastery are dedicated falls on a Wednesday or Friday, that feast is counted with the great feasts of the Lord, and we eat fish twice a day and drink wine. See the Great Typikon, ch. 32.

== On great feasts
On great feasts there is no kneeling at all and no fasting, either in church or in the cells. If such a feast falls on a Wednesday or Friday, we allow fish, oil and wine, twice a day. The exceptions are the Exaltation of the Precious Cross and the Beheading of the Precious Head of the Forerunner. On those days we do not eat fish, whatever day of the week they fall on, but we eat with oil and drink wine. In the afterfeast of such feasts there are no prostrations in church, even in the middle of a fast. In private, both are made. See Nikon, discourse 57.
